**Cascade at a fitted cut.** Laya's yes/no scores sit away from 0.5, so `verdict cascade` can use a cut fitted on your own labels, twice: to decide, and to send the answers nearest it to the second server. On five public yes/no suites, Laya alone went from 0.814 to 0.860 on held-out items (every suite improved), and the cascade with 30% sent from 0.872 to 0.916 (FINDINGS §55).

- `verdict cascade --check LABELLED.jsonl --fit [--out FIT.json]` fits each cut on 70% of your examples and reports the held-out 30%.
- `verdict cascade --calibration FIT.json` decides at the fitted cut and sends the answers nearest it (use `--share`, not `--below`).
- Docs and help now say 0.5 is the wrong line for Laya's yes/no, and `verdict calibrate` is the first step before gating on a score.
- `python -m verdict.router_eval --refit` defaults to base Laya.

Measured and not built (FINDINGS §55 to §57): asking the same question another way, conformal prediction sets, and a few-shot head on Laya's encoder. None beat Laya's own probabilities or the fitted cut. The scorecard is identical to v0.7.1's (no model change).

Install: `uv tool install --python 3.11 'verdict[mlx,laya] @ git+https://github.com/iksnerd/verdict.git@v0.8.0'`, or `verdict update`.
