Two opt-in features, a fix for how a lazily loaded engine reports its limit, and a move to laya 0.5.3 and laya-mlx 0.8.1. Nothing changes unless you ask: the scorecard is identical to v0.10.3, and `decide --jsonl` rows keep their shape.

**`--abstain-below P`: mark the answers that are not sure**

- `verdict decide` (including `--jsonl`) and `verdict ask --json` take `--abstain-below P`, and `POST /v1/decide` takes `?abstain_below=P`. Each answer then carries `abstention` (`passed` or `abstained`) and `abstention_threshold`. The keys are absent when you do not ask.
- An answer abstains when its largest probability is below `P`. For a yes/no that is the larger of the yes and no sides. It reports only: the answer itself is not changed, and your script decides what an abstained row means.
- An answer from the `uniform` backend, which means "no model", is marked `unevaluated`, never `passed` or `abstained`.
- A base Laya probability is over-confident, so `P` is a threshold, not a calibrated chance. Pick it from labelled data with `verdict calibrate`.
- `/v1/systemone` and `/v1/route` are unchanged.

**`[model].max_len`: read more of a long state**

- `[model].max_len` (also `$VERDICT_MAX_LEN` and `--max-len` on `serve` and the commands that ask) raises how many tokens the checkpoint reads. The default, 0, keeps the checkpoint's own limit (512 English, 1024 multilingual). It takes 0 or 6 to 8192 and is refused if it is not above the checkpoint's `head_max_len`.
- It costs what it reads: about 0.09 s a call at 512, 0.4 s at 2048, 3.2 s at 8192. The checkpoint was trained at 512, so accuracy beyond that is unmeasured and calibration does not carry over. In a probe of eight invented items, the decisive sentence at the end of a 1,500-token thread registered only at 2048, and the "no" items still scored 0.77 to 0.87 yes. FINDINGS §64 has the numbers.
- `prompt_token_budget` clips the state first, so raise it too (or set it to 0). verdict warns when `max_len` would have no effect.
- Scorecards and calibration fits record `max_len` only when it is not the default, so every earlier scorecard still verifies. A fit made at another `max_len` warns.

**Fixed**

- A lazily loaded multilingual engine reported a 512-token limit instead of its real 1024, so it marked states of 513 to 1024 tokens as truncated when the model read them whole. The limit is now worked out once, on first use, and then fixed.

**Dependencies**

- The lock moves to laya 0.5.3 and laya-mlx 0.8.1 (from 0.3.20 and 0.5.0), which adds `cryptography`, `cffi` and `vegaml` to the install. The suite passes and the base-Laya scorecard is identical to v0.10.3.
- laya 0.5 also reads images, audio and video, takes 73k tokens, and can learn from confirmed answers. verdict does not use those: they need laya's torch path and a separate reader model, not the MLX encoder verdict runs.

Install: `uv tool install --python 3.11 'verdict[mlx,laya] @ git+https://github.com/iksnerd/verdict.git@v0.11.0'`, or `verdict update`.
